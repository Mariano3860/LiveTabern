# Sustaining the closed tavern economy

Status: accepted for the local simulation.

The original seven-day conservation test concealed an economic dead end. With the default seed and one-second steps, purchases declined from 243 on day one to 86 on day two and zero from day three onward. All 345 initial coins eventually belonged to Borin. Duets stopped after day two because the performers could no longer manage thirst, while unused daily ale deliveries accumulated to 1,591 servings. Conservation alone did not establish a living economy.

Each observed UTC hour now tops the reserve up to 240 servings, adding only the actual delivery to both stock and provenance. Existing oversized reserves are retained and consumed before new supply is needed. The process does not multiply deliveries by skipped hours. Supply is an explicit external resource; currency remains a closed system.

Borin redistributes up to three existing coins to each of the fourteen regulars every observed hour. Everyone receives the same whole-coin amount; the calculation preserves at least four coins for the host and never overdraws his purse. The event describes sharing takings, rather than inventing a completed work action. A persisted hourly watermark prevents duplicate disbursements after recovery. Older snapshots acquire a watermark of -1 and execute at most one current-hour maintenance pass at the next tick, with no retrospective award for elapsed days.

Thirst grows at 0.035 per simulation second rather than 0.18. A serving still reduces thirst by 45. Everyday action choice, time injection, role/personality, price, payment, stock checks, and seeded consent remain unchanged. Trade stock is finite and replenishing it is separate future work. This is a game economy policy, not a real-world economic model.

Under the same seven-day analysis, day seven records 994 purchases, 3,709 conversations, 4,058 music events, and 433 shared-performance beginnings. Music events include individual starts and the shared duet event. Total coins remain 345, tonic ownership remains 24, and fresh-world ale stock stays at or below 240. Regression checks require purchases, conversation, music, and duets on every day, not just somewhere during the week. These are accelerated logic results, not production uptime or human playtesting.
